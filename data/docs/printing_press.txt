The Printing Press

Around 1440 the German goldsmith Johannes Gutenberg invented the movable-type printing press in the city of Mainz. Earlier printing in Asia used carved wooden blocks or ceramic type, but Gutenberg combined a metal type alloy, oil-based ink and a screw press adapted from wine making.

His most famous work, the Gutenberg Bible, was printed in about 1455. Books, previously copied by hand by monks and scribes, could now be produced quickly and cheaply. Printing spread to more than two hundred European cities by 1500.

The press helped spread literacy, supported the Renaissance and the Protestant Reformation, and allowed scientific ideas to be shared widely. It is often counted among the most important inventions of the second millennium.
