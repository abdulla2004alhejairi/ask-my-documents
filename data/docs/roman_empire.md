# The Roman Empire

The Roman Empire began in 27 BC when Octavian was given the title Augustus by the Senate, ending the Roman Republic. Augustus became the first Roman emperor and brought about the Pax Romana, a long period of relative peace and stability across the Mediterranean world.

At its greatest extent under the emperor Trajan, around 117 AD, the empire stretched from Britain to Mesopotamia and surrounded the whole Mediterranean Sea. Rome built roads, aqueducts and cities, and Latin became the common language of the western provinces.

In 285 AD Diocletian divided the administration into eastern and western halves. The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 AD when the Germanic leader Odoacer deposed the last emperor, Romulus Augustulus. The Eastern Roman Empire, also called the Byzantine Empire, survived until Constantinople fell to the Ottomans in 1453.
